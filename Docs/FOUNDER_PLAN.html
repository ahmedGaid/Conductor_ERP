Sections 3 – 8

Six phases, six gates

Each phase has an entry condition, a job, exit metrics, and a funding event. You do not start a phase early — skipping gates is how companies die looking busy.

2026 – 2027 Product & proof You are here Bootstrap → YC / pre-seed

Finish the ARP core and put ten real companies on it. The month-close running live at a real customer is the launch moment of the company — not just a feature.

Key moves

  • Finish AI workspace sessions 01–15 (in flight).
  • Migration agent — spreadsheets → running company in one afternoon. The go-to-market weapon, not a feature: ERP dies at implementation; ours takes an afternoon.
  • Autonomous month-close — the flagship. Its first live run opens the claims gate and unlocks the public ARP story.
  • Pick one ICP and write it down: Egyptian trading & distribution SMBs (10–100 staff) that already use an external accountant.
  • Onboard all ten pilots personally. Payment = data, weekly calls, permission to tell their story.

Exit gate

  • Month-close ran live, audit trail complete → public ARP claim
  • 10 companies live · ≥7 open it every working day
  • Real messy migration ≤ one afternoon, zero consultant hours
  • Written ICP one-pager; all ten pilots match it

2027 – 2028 Product-market fit Seed ~$1–2M

Prove one type of company will pay, stay, and refer — repeatedly. 100 customers is still founder-scale: talk to every one personally; every churn gets a written post-mortem sentence.

Key moves

  • Convert pilots to paid. Simple public pricing, EGP tiers, annual pays 10 months, no custom deals.
  • Grow 10 → 50 → 100 paying. Channels in order: pilot referrals → their external accountants → Arabic demo-driven content (YouTube, LinkedIn) — demos, never hype.
  • Ship retention features: daily brief, anomaly watch, bank photo reconciliation (roadmap C–D). Scope freeze holds: no HR, no manufacturing.
  • First 3 hires only after ~50 paying: support engineer → full-stack engineer → part-time finance. Culture one-pager written before hire #1.

Systems that run through every phase

Brand, revenue layers, the data moat, the trust ladder, and the founder's own ladder — none of these is a phase; all of them compound across all six.

10–30Manager — hire, write the playbooks, run the metrics week

100–300Executive — strategy, allocation, key partners, category story

The data & AI moat — the compounding asset

Every phase feeds it: migration sees every messy company → month-close sees every book → anomaly watch learns every fraud pattern → bank vision reads every statement format → WhatsApp hears every question in Egyptian Arabic. Rules that keep it an asset: explicit granular consent · anonymized aggregates only · the six ARP mechanics never weaken · public AI-responsibility page from Phase 3. Trust is value #1 — the moat is only a moat if customers brag about giving us the data.

Sections 14 – 15

Risks named now, refusals written down

Reviewed quarterly. Every phase's plan must be survivable at 60% of the target raise; default-alive after Phase 3 is the standing goal.

Egypt macro / currencyGCC revenue from Phase 4 is the hard-currency hedge; costs stay in EGP.
Incumbents copy "AI features"They can bolt on chat; they can't retrofit actor-scoped, auditable agentic architecture. Keep the six mechanics inviolable, keep shipping demos.
Founder burnout / bus factorThe founder ladder + write-everything-down + a leadership layer at Phase 4.
AI cost / quality shiftsModel-agnostic agent layer; usage-based pricing passes cost through.
Compliance surpriseE-invoicing rule changes are product features with an owner, not chores — budgeted every phase.
Fundraising winterGates don't move for money weather; the plan survives at 60% of each raise.

The company-level refuse list — what we will not do

  1. No phase-skipping. No Series A before repeatable sales, no KSA before PMF, no marketplace before partners exist.
  2. No revenue that fights the product — no agency business, no custom forks, no white-labeling that hides the brand.
  3. No feature-grid selling, no hype claims — demos or silence, at every company size.
  4. No hiring ahead of proof. Headcount follows revenue evidence, never precedes it.
  5. No market entry without the checklist and a named owner.
