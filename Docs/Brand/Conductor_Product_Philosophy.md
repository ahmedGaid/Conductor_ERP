## Mission
Give an Egyptian owner calm, correct, Arabic-first command of the whole business — software so
good to use that a new person does **real work within minutes, without training** (Brief §14), and
trusts the numbers because they are correct by construction. We do not compete on feature count. We
compete on **the feeling of using the software** — the one thing almost no ERP competes on (Brief §2).
*"Your business, conducted with precision." — أعمالك، بإتقان.*

## The Conductor Standard — the 8 (full text: Directive)
The ship test. Fail one and the feature is not done. (Each points to where its real rules live.)
1. **Invisible complexity** — depth hidden behind a simple interaction; hide, never omit.
2. **Instant performance** — every action feels immediate; saving is invisible.
3. **Calm by default** — no noise, no needless alerts; monochrome chrome.
4. **Trust through transparency** — say what will happen *before*, reassure *after*; reversible.
5. **Consistency everywhere** — the same action behaves the same on every screen.
6. **Human AI** — notices, explains, protects; proactive, never surprising (gated, Brief §12).
7. **Craft in every detail** — type, spacing, motion, language, errors feel deliberately designed.
8. **Business confidence** — the user leaves every screen more informed and more in control.

## Seven questions to decide anything
Not new rules — the Standard, re-asked as you work. Any "no" means keep going.
1. Is this the **one obvious thing** on the screen, with one primary action? *(→ Standard 1)*
2. Does it feel **instant** — no wait, no spinner where cached data could paint? *(→ Standard 2)*
3. Is the chrome still **calm and monochrome**, and does any colour **earn its place** with a word/icon? *(→ Standard 3)*
4. Does it say **what will happen before**, and **reassure after** — is it reversible? *(→ Standard 4)*
5. Does the **Arabic read like a person wrote it** — one canonical word, human status, blame-free error? *(→ Standard 7; Identity §6)*
6. Does it make the business **easier** — user leaves more in control, not more burdened? *(→ Standard 8)*
7. **Would Linear ship this?** An honest "no" is not done. *(→ Brief §2)*

## Where AI stands
AI is a **business partner, not a chatbot**: it *notices, explains, predicts, recommends, teaches,
and protects* — turning "Ahmed hasn't paid recently" into the aging view, a drafted reminder, and a
payment plan one click away. Always **drafts-only, reversible, transparent** — never a raw chat box
bolted on, never acting without a person confirming. It is a **design north-star, gated by claims
discipline** (Brief §12): **not marketed before it ships**, and when it ships it is *assistive,
optional, and transparent* — never "automatic." (Directive §"Experience north-stars"; Standard 6.)

## Premium means confidence
Our luxury is **confidence, simplicity, and silence — never flash** (Brief §2). Reassurance *is*
premium UX (Directive §5). "More premium" never means more colour, more animation, or more chrome;
it means the owner is **less afraid to click** and **more sure the numbers are right**. If a change
does not make the product calmer, clearer, or more trustworthy, it is not an upgrade.

## Conductor has taste
Taste here is **restraint, not decoration**: one voice of type, one hand of icons, one canonical
Arabic word per concept, designed empty/error/loading states, settled motion, near-black chrome.
It means making the deliberate choice and removing the rest — so the product feels designed by
people who care about every detail. Taste already has a name in each pillar; this doc just remembers
to look.

## Where to go next
- **Copy, naming, positioning, marketing, claims** → the **Brief**.
- **Any screen, component, state, token, motion, a11y, or i18n rule** → the **Directive** (it wins).
- **Logo, type, colour meaning, icons, the Arabic lexicon, invoice/email/web/OG** → the **Identity System**.
- **"Is this actually on-brand?"** → run the `conductor-brand` brand-feel checklist (judgment a gate can't see).
